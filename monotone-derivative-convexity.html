A Nondecreasing Derivative Implies Convexity

A mean-value-theorem proof of the convexity inequality.

Dale Matthews

Proposition. Let \(\varphi:(a,b)\to\mathbb{R}\) be differentiable. If \(\varphi'\) is nondecreasing on \((a,b)\), then \(\varphi\) is convex on \((a,b)\).

Proof

Suppose \(a<s<t<u<b\). By the mean value theorem there are points \(c_1\in(s,t)\) and \(c_2\in(t,u)\) such that

\[ \frac{\varphi(t)-\varphi(s)}{t-s}=\varphi'(c_1) \quad\text{and}\quad \frac{\varphi(u)-\varphi(t)}{u-t}=\varphi'(c_2). \]

Since \(c_1<c_2\) and \(\varphi'\) is nondecreasing,

\[ \frac{\varphi(t)-\varphi(s)}{t-s} \leq \frac{\varphi(u)-\varphi(t)}{u-t}. \]

Multiplying by the positive quantity \((t-s)(u-t)\) and rearranging gives

\[ \begin{aligned} (\varphi(t)-\varphi(s))(u-t) &\leq (\varphi(u)-\varphi(t))(t-s), \\ \varphi(t)(u-s) &\leq \varphi(s)(u-t)+\varphi(u)(t-s), \end{aligned} \]

and therefore

\[ \varphi(t) \leq \varphi(s)\frac{u-t}{u-s} +\varphi(u)\frac{t-s}{u-s}. \]

Let

\[ \lambda=\frac{t-s}{u-s}. \]

Then \(0<\lambda<1\), and

\[ 1-\lambda=\frac{u-t}{u-s} \quad\text{and}\quad (1-\lambda)s+\lambda u=t. \]

As \(t\) ranges between \(s\) and \(u\), \(\lambda\) ranges over \((0,1)\).

It follows that

\[ \varphi((1-\lambda)s+\lambda u) \leq (1-\lambda)\varphi(s)+\lambda\varphi(u). \]

For \(\lambda=0\) or \(\lambda=1\), the same inequality is an equality. Thus \(\varphi\) is convex on \((a,b)\). \(\square\)
