Why the Dyadic Approximations Are Increasing

A three-case verification for the truncated dyadic-floor approximations.

Dale Matthews

For each positive integer \(n\), put \(\delta_n=2^{-n}\). For \(0\leq t<n\), let \(k_n(t)\) be the unique integer satisfying \(k_n(t)\delta_n\leq t<(k_n(t)+1)\delta_n\), and define

\[ \varphi_n(t)= \begin{cases} k_n(t)\delta_n, & 0\leq t<n, \\ n, & n\leq t\leq\infty. \end{cases} \]

Proposition. For every \(t\in[0,\infty]\), \(\varphi_n(t)\leq\varphi_{n+1}(t)\).

Proof

There are three cases.

1. \(0\leq t<n\)

The value \(\varphi_n(t)\) is the largest multiple of \(2^{-n}\) that is not greater than \(t\), while \(\varphi_{n+1}(t)\) is the largest multiple of \(2^{-(n+1)}\) that is not greater than \(t\).

Since

\[ k_n(t)2^{-n}=2k_n(t)2^{-(n+1)}, \]

every multiple of \(2^{-n}\) is also a multiple of \(2^{-(n+1)}\). Therefore

\[ \varphi_n(t)\leq\varphi_{n+1}(t). \]

2. \(n\leq t<n+1\)

Here \(\varphi_n(t)=n\). Also, \(\varphi_{n+1}(t)\) is the largest multiple of \(2^{-(n+1)}\) that is not greater than \(t\). Since

\[ n=\bigl(n2^{n+1}\bigr)2^{-(n+1)}, \]

the number \(n\) is such a multiple, and \(n\leq t\). Hence

\[ \varphi_n(t)=n\leq\varphi_{n+1}(t). \]

3. \(n+1\leq t\leq\infty\)

In this case,

\[ \varphi_n(t)=n\leq n+1=\varphi_{n+1}(t). \]

Thus in all three cases \(\varphi_n(t)\leq\varphi_{n+1}(t)\). \(\square\)
