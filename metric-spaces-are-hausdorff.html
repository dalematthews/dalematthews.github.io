Every Metric Space Is Hausdorff

Distinct points in a metric space have disjoint open-ball neighborhoods.

Dale Matthews

Proposition. Every metric space is a Hausdorff space.

Proof

Let \(p\) and \(q\) be distinct points in a metric space, and let

\[ r=\frac{1}{2}d(p,q). \]

Then \(U=B_r(p)\) and \(V=B_r(q)\) are neighborhoods of \(p\) and \(q\), respectively, and

\[ U\cap V=\varnothing. \]

Therefore every pair of distinct points has disjoint neighborhoods, so the metric space is Hausdorff. \(\square\)
