Counting Points in a Refined Dyadic Grid

The number of fine-grid points inside an aligned half-open dyadic box follows from the ratio of the two side lengths.

Dale Matthews

Proposition. Let \(k,n,r\) be positive integers with \(n>r\). For each positive integer \(m\), let \(P_m\) be the points of \(\mathbb{R}^k\) whose coordinates are integral multiples of \(2^{-m}\). Let \(Q\) be an aligned, half-open dyadic box of side length \(2^{-r}\); that is, for some \(a\in P_r\), let \(Q=\prod_{j=1}^k [a_j,a_j+2^{-r})\). Then \(P_n\) has exactly \(2^{(n-r)k}\) points in \(Q\).

Proof

Since \(n>r\), a dyadic box of side length \(2^{-n}\) is smaller than one of side length \(2^{-r}\). The ratio of their side lengths is

\[ \frac{2^{-r}}{2^{-n}}=2^{n-r}. \]

Thus there are \(2^{n-r}\) fine dyadic boxes along each of the \(k\) coordinate directions, and hence there are

\[ \left(2^{n-r}\right)^k=2^{(n-r)k} \]

dyadic boxes of side length \(2^{-n}\) inside \(Q\). Each such half-open box contributes one point of \(P_n\) lying in \(Q\), namely its corner. Therefore \(P_n\) has exactly \(2^{(n-r)k}\) points in \(Q\). \(\square\)
